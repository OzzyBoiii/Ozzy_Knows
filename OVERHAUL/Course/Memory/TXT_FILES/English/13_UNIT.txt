Grammar
<p>
                <!---->
                Articles:no article, a/an or the <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - We do NOT use an article:
                - when we are talking about something in general, before uncountable nouns or before plural countable -nouns: Primary education is compulsory in this country.
                Farmers in poor countries earn very little.
                - before the names of continents and most countries and towns:
                in Europe, in Ireland, in Dublin
                Exceptions: in the United States, in the United Kingdom, in the Netherlands, in The Hague
                We use a/an:
                - when we mention something for the first time or to refer to any one of a kind or group: London is a big city. (one of many big cities in the world)
                - when talking about someone's job:
                Lucy is a doctor.
                We use the:
                - before singular and plural countable nouns, to refer to something that we have mentioned before: He lived in a big city. The city was polluted.
                - to refer to something specific or unique:
                I like the colour of that shirt.
                Who is the Prime Minister of your country?
                - to refer to a period in history: the Renaissance
                with the superlative forms of adjectives:
                the worst
                - with ordinal numbers:
                the third
                <!---->
                non-definining relative clauses <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - give additional information about the person, thing, place, etc. we are talking about. The sentence still makes sense without this information:
                Giant pandas, which are an endangered species, live in the mountains in central China. → Giant pandas live in the mountains in central China.
                ⚫ are always separated from the rest of the sentence using commas,
                ⚫ start with relative pronouns who, which, where or whose (but NOT that):
                Last year we visited our friends on Aran Islands, where you can see lots of bird species.
                We can't leave out the relative pronouns in non-defining relative clauses.
              </p>

                Prepositions at the end of clauses <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                We put a preposition at the end of clauses: ⚫in relative clauses:
                This is the scientist I have been waiting for.
                Camping by a lake is something we're enthusiastic about.
                There are several environmental issues you have to prepare a presentation about.
                ⚫ in wh- questions:
                When creating a detailed question in which a verb or an adjective is followed by a preposition and a noun, such
                as listen to music, afraid of spiders, etc., we leave the preposition after the verb or adjective.
                What kind of animals are you afraid of?
                What are you good at?
                ⚫ in infinitive structures:
                Cape Horn is probably the most hazardous place to sail around.
                Here are the safety procedures you need to be aware of.

  <p>
                'for and against'/argumentative essay<br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - used to report on the positive and negative sides of a particular topic
                - GENERAL INFORMATION<br>
                - 200-250 words
                - 4 paragraphs (introduction, body (2 paragraphs within it (argument for, argument against)), conclusion)<br>
                - BEFORE WRITTING<br>
                - Write down the prompt
                - Define the topic
                - Define thesis statements
                - write down at least 2 arguments for and 2 against<br>
                - Write down the linkers (words that begin ur paragraphs, arguments and topic sentences)<br>
              </p>

              <p>
                - PROMPT (EXAMPLE)<br>
                - Some say it would be better if there was only one language in the world. Others disagree.<br>
                - INTRODUCTION<br>
                - General statement <br>
                – introduce the topic              
                - "Language is the easiest form of communication and what distinguishes us from animals."
                - Thesis statement = 2 points of view
                - Paraphraze the prompt instead of lifting/copying it<br>
                - "It is believed it would be much easier to communicate if we all spoke the same language while many think it would destroy cultural diversity."<br>
                - BODY (2 PARAGRAPHS)<br>
                - Topic sentence = generally about the paragraph<br>
                -"On one hand, having a single language would bring numerous benefits."<br>
                - At least 2 arguments with their explanation<br>
                - Support = an example that justifies and explains the argument
                - "Firstly, communication among different nations would become much simpler and easier. There would be no need for learning foreign languages in order to do business or travel abroad. Secondly,…"<br>
                - CONCLUSION<br>
                - Summary = must result from the body (the previous two paragraphs) 
                - Personal opinion
                -"To sum up, even though introducing a common language would improve the life quality  we would most certainly lose our cultural diversity. In my opinion,…"<br>
                - GRADING<br>
                - Task completion = on topic, structure, argumentation, support<br>
                - Coherence and cohesion = flow of ideas, cohesive devices, paragraphing<br>
                - Vocabulary = range and accuracy<br>
                - Grammar = range and accuracy <br>
                - the N.O.S.E 
                -  Neutral–Use a neutral writing style. Avoid using informal language (for example, don’t say „kids”, say „children”) and grammatical structures. Contractions are not strictly forbidden, but some should be avoided if at all possible (for example „hadn’t”, „must’ve”, „You’ll”, etc.) It’s not a big mistake if you DO use them, but with time try to phase them out.  Objective–You can’t show that you favour one side of the argument over the other inany way. This is something that students often have problems with, especially if the topic in question is something they feel very strongly about. There’s nothing else to say here but: suck it up. Remember, just because you’re writing about something you disagree with doesn’t automatically mean that you agree with it.  Succinct/Simple– Avoid wordiness (or tautology). It’s typically used by students who fear that they will appear as less intelligent if they use everyday words, but then overcompensate and use a totally unnecessarily complex (or superfluous) word. You can use them sparingly, but avoid overloading your essay with them. A good rule of thumb is to not use two words when one word will do.  Effective– Some students feel that if they overload their essays with 5 arguments for each side that then the reader will think they’re really really smart. We won’t. The only thing which will probably happen is that you’ll run out of good arguments and use poor arguments which aren’t really well explained in your essay. It’s better to have fever arguments which are well explained and supported than a bunch of them which you won’t coverwell enough
                <!---->
                - Paragraph 1 - Introduction<br>
                - Begin with general comments or facts about the topic.<br>
                - End with a statement that mentions both sides of the issue.<br>
                - Paragraph 2 - Arguments for<br>
                - Present two or three arguments for the topic.<br>
                - Include some examples to support arguments.<br>
                - Paragraph 3 - Arguments against<br>
                - Write a similar paragraph presenting and supporting arguments against the topic.<br>
                - Paragraph 4 - Conclusion<br>
                - Make a summarising statement.<br>
                - Add your personal opinion.

                Linkers 
                List arguments: (1) First of all, Firstly, Secondly, (2) , In addition, Finally
                Give examples: For example, (3)  
                Show contrast: However, (4) , On the other hand
                Give a personal opinion: In my opinion, (5)  
                Introduce a conclusion: In conclusion, (6)
