Grammar
 <p>
                <!---->
                First Conditional <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - used to talk about the possible results of an action 
                - sentences refer to the future 
                - "If I tell them the truth, they will not believe me." 
                - To form First Conditional, we have 2 choices 
                - Formation A: condition (If + Present Simple) + comma (,) + result (will (not) + infinitive) 
                - "If he works hard, he will pass his exams." 
                - Formation B: result (will (not) + infinitive) + condition (If + Present Simple) 
                - "He will pass his exams if he works hard."

                Defining relative clauses <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - we define them to determine essential information about a person, thing or place following relative pronouns 
                - who and that 
                - used to talk about people 
                - "This is the teacher who/that teaches my class." 
                - which and that - used to talk about things 
                - "Is this the laptop which/that you ordered?" 
                - where - used to talk about places 
                - "Ella went to a school where most puplis were girls." 
                - all of these relative pronouns usually come after the noun (person/thing/place) they refer to

Language
 <p>
                <!---->
                Future time and conditional clauses 
                - Conditional clauses 
                - introduced with these examples 
                - if ("If Mark does not hand in his homework today, his teacher will get really angry.") 
                - unless ("Amy will not make any progress unless she works systematically.") 
                - Time clauses 
                - introduced with these examples 
                - when ("When the new term starts, the teachers will meet up.") 
                - before ("Before i take Chemistry and Physics for my A levels, I will talk to my teachers.") 
                - after ("After you mark your homework, you will see your mistakes.") 
                - as soon as ("The kids will feel better as soon as the school breaks up for holiday.") 
                - Present Simple is used in future times and conditional clauses 
                - formation: Subordinate clause (if/unless/when/before/after/as soon as + Present Simple) + Main clause (will (not) + verb) 
                - "My sister will never succeed unless she learns from her mistakes." 
                - Either clause may come first in the sentence 
                - We put a comma at the end of the subordinate clause if it comes first in the sentence 
                - "As soon as I get my new timetable, I will let you know about the time of our meeting." 
                - We do not put a comma at the end of the mainclause if it comes first in the sentence 
                - "I will let you know about the time of our meeting as soon as I get my new timetable."

                Indirect questions <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - used in formal letters and emails to be more formal 
                - Formation; subject + verb (+object) 
                - "How long will I eed to study?" = "Could you tell me how long will I need to study etc." 
                - when there is no question word in a direct question, you use if or whether in the indirect question 
                - "Can you arrange accommodation for me?" = "If you could arrage accommodation for me etc."

Writting
 <p> 
                <!---->
                An enquiry <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - Start the email with Dear Mr or Mrs and the person"s surname (if you do not know person"s surname, use Sir or Madam) 
                - instead of abbreviations or contractions use full forms 
                - "I would etc." (not i"d) 
                - Use formal expressions to 
                - say why you are writing the email 
                - "I am writting to etc." 
                - ask for information 
                - "I would also like to know if etc." 
                - ask somebody to do something for you 
                - "I would be grateful if etc." 
                - say that you expect a reply 
                - "I look forward to etc." 
                - Finish the email with "Yours sincerely"
