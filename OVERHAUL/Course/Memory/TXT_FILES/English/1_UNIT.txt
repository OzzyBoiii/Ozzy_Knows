###TEXT2###
Language
 <p>
                <!---->
                So and such <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - either used when we want to stress the noun they precede or when we want to stress an adjective or noun and focus on the consequences of events described in the main sentence clause <br>
                - "My younger sister has got so many toys that my parents want to give some away." <br>
                - pronoun so <br>
                - formation = so + adjective <br>
                - It is used before <br>
                - adjectives without nouns 
                - "My aunt Tanya is so inspiring." <br>
                - quantifying expressions (many/much) followed by a noun 
                - "So many friends are coming to the party." <br>
                - pronoun such <br>
                - formation = such + noun phrase <br>
                - It is used before <br>
                - adjectives followed by nouns <br>
                - the following nouns: <br>
                - "Such people will always help you in need." <br>
                - "Angela always has such luck." <br>
                - Information about it <br>
                - the indefinite article a/an is used if it precedes a countable singular noun <br>
                - "Uncle Tom has such an intersting life." <br>
                - we do not use any article if it precedes a countable singular form <br>
                - "Every morning Josh goes for such long walks with his dogs." <br>
                <!---->
                Adjective + preposition <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - used to give information about yourself <br>
                - I am crazy/excited/mad/passionate/serious/worried about <br>
                - I am bad/good/useless at <br>
                - I am involved in <br>
                - I am afraid of <br>
                - I am keen on <br>
                - I am dissapointed/obsessed with <br>
                - Questions can end in a proposition <br>
                - What subject are you good at?<br>

###TEXT3###
Writting
 <p>
                <!---->
                A pearsonal email/letter (OVERVIEWED)<br>
                - start writting with a friendly greeting (Dear Nick,/Hi Jo) <br>
                - do not use full forms, use contractions (you"re (not you are)/I'm) <br>
                - occasionally use emotions (💀) or abbreviations (Bye 4 now) <br>
                - ask questions to show you want a reply (What about you?) <br>
                - finish writting with a friendly goodbye (All the best/Bye 4 now)<br>
              </p>
