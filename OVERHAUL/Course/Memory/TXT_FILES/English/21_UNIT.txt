Grammar
p>

                &bull; Reported Speech<br> (OVERVIEWED)
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; In Reported Speech we change the following: <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; time expressions and words referring to places (depending on the context)<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; demonstrative pronouns, personal pronouns, object pronouns and possessive adjectives (depending on the context) <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; tenses in affirmative sentences and questions (only if the reporting verb is in the past tense (e.g. he said, she told me, he asked)).<br> 
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; If the reporting verb is in the present tense (e.g. Present Simple or Present Perfect), we do not change the tense in the reported statement: <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘I’m not satisfied with your performance.’ ➙ The teacher has said she is not satisfied with my performance.<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘How do you handle stress?’ ➙ She wants to know how I handle stress. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; If the reporting verb is in the past tense, we do not change the following in the reported statement:<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verbs in the Past Perfect Simple and Past Perfect Continuous:<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull;‘Before my resignation I had been working on an environmental project.’ ➙ The candidate said that before his resignation he had been working on an environmental project.<br> 
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull;‘Had you ever been to a job interview before that last one?’ ➙ I asked my neighbour if she had ever been to a job interview before that last one.<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; modal verbs would, should, could, might, must:<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘During the interview you should try to make eye contact.’ ➙ The advisor told me that during the interview I should try to make eye contact. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘We must all try harder.’ ➙ The manager said that we must all try harder.<br>
                <!---->
                &bull; Reporting verbs (besides say and tell)<br> (OVERVIEWED)
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; Verbs without a direct object<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull;verb + (that) (e.g.: add, admit, agree, claim, complain, deny, explain, insist, promise, propose, recommend, regret, suggest):<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘I wasted a lot of money.’ ➙ Sue complained (that) she had wasted a lot of money.<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verb + (not) to + infinitive (e.g.: agree, claim, decide, offer, promise, refuse, threaten):<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘I’ll hire two new employees.’ ➙ The manager decided to hire two new employees.<br> 
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verb + (not) -ing (e.g.: admit, deny, propose, recommend, regret, suggest):<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘I didn’t take the money.’ ➙ The cashier denied taking the money.<br> 
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verb + preposition + (not) -ing (e.g.: apologise for, insist on, object to):<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;‘I’m sorry I was rude.’ ➙ I apologised for being rude.<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; Verbs with a direct object<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verb + object + (that) (e.g.: advise, assure, convince, inform, persuade, promise, remind, tell, warn):<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘The meeting will start in an hour.’ ➞ The secretary reminded them (that) the meeting would start in an hour.<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verb + object + (not) to + infinitive (e.g.: advise, ask, beg, convince, encourage, invite, order, persuade, recommend, remind, tell, warn, urge):<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘You really should apply for this job.’ ➞ Everybody encouraged me to apply for that job.<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verb + object + preposition + (not) -ing (e.g.: accuse sb of, blame sb for, criticise sb for, congratulate sb on, praise sb for, suspect sb of, thank sb for: )<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ‘It’s your fault that the car was damaged.’ ➞ Joe’s boss blames him for damaging the car.<br>

                &bull; Phrasal verbs (advanced points)<br> (OVERVIEWED)
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; Three-part phrasal verbs <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; verbs formed with a common verb and a variety of adverbial and propositional particles:<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Tom dropped out of school after failing his exams. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; You should stand up for what you believe in. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Kate couldn't keep up with other students. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; They are inseparable (the object (a noun or pronoun) follows the whole phrasal verb): <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; We are sure to come up against a lot of opposition. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; We are sure to come up a lot of opposition against <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; Word building with phrasal verbs <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; Nouns (the stress falls on the 'particle' part)<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; verb + adverbial/prepositional particle (clear out- a clear-out, let sb down- a letdown, warm sth up- a warm-up)<br> 
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; I had a clear-out because I needed more space for some new equipment. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; A friend of mine took a year out to avoid burnout. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; In such words, the stress falls on the verb part. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; adverbial and prepositional particle + verb (bring up→ upbringing, pour down→ downpour, set out → outset, put in→ input) <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Please, allow me to say a few words at the outset. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; They had the greatest input into our company <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; Adjectives<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; adverbial/prepositional particle + verb + -ing (put off→ off-putting, lift up→ uplifting, stand out → outstanding) <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Our teacher told us an uplifting story. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; For such outstanding work, you deserve a pay rise.<br> 
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; verb (Past Participle) + adverbial/prepositional particle (run down→ run-down, build up→ built-up, wear out→→ worn-out)<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; You are driving through a built-up area. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The boys grow up in this run-down building you see.<br> 
                Note: remember to check the spelling of such nouns and adjectives in a dictionary before you use them."<br>

                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; This essay will look at this question from the perspective of... <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; &bull; Paragraphs 2 and 3 <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; Discuss different aspects of the issue and support the main opinion you stated in the introduction. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull; Expand the discussion by giving examples. <br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; When it comes to...<br>
                &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; It is also important to consider this issue with regards to... <br>
