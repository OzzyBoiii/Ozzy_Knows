                Narrative tenses<br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - Past Continuous
                - used to set the scene
                - "... athletes were competing in a 20-kilometre race in Kenya."
                - Past Simple
                - used to describe the main events of a story
                - "He did not speed up - but then he looked round and saw ..."
                - Past Continuous with the Past Simple
                - used when a short action (Past Simple) interrupted a longer unfinished action (Past Continuous)
                - "He was leading when suddenly, a local runner overtook him."
                - Past Perfect
                - used to make it clear that one past action happened before another past action
                - "He saw that a large rhinoceros had crashed through the trees" 
                <!---->
                Verb patterns <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - verbs which follow other verbs in the next patterns
                - (not) to + infinitive
                - after aim, arrange, attempt, decide, expect, hope, intend, manage, offer, plan, refuse, remember, seem, tend, try, want
                - "they decided not to work with a sports psychologist 
                - object + (not) to + infinitive
                - after advise, allow, encourage, force, remind, teach, urge, warn
                - "She taught me to believe in myself
                - -ing
                - after avoid, cannot help, cannot stand, do not mind, enjoy, fancy, finish, imagine, keep, miss, stop, waste time
                - "Just imagine winning the competition"
                - infinitive
                - after modal verbs (can, could, might, should, would
                - "You should answer his question right now"
                - object + infinitive
                - after make, left
                - "they let me visit the stadium"

Language
<p>
                <!---->
                so, too, neither/nor, either <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - usage
                - used in short statements meaning (me) too or (me) neither
                - used in reactions to somebody's statements to let the other person know that we are in the same situation
                - so and too
                - used in positive statements
                - 
                - structures of the short statements are the following
                - so + auxiliary/modal verb + subject (noun/pronoun)
                - "our football team has let us down recently. So has ours."
                - "I should go jogging every day. So should I."
                - subject (noun/pronoun) + auxiliary/modal verb + too
                - "Michael broke his personal record yesterday. You did too."
                - neither/nor and either
                - used to say something is the same or agree with a negative statement
                - structures of the short statements are the following
                - neither/nor + auxiliary/modal verb in a negative form + either
                - "My coach is not going to be pleased with my score. Neither/Nor is mine."
                - "Jerry will never give up running marathons. Neither/Nor will I"
                - subject (noun/pronoun) + auxiliary/modal verb in a negative form + either
                - "She did not play for the school team last year. I did not either.
                - exceptions
                - Present Simple
                - the auxiliary verb is do/does
                - "Rob takes part in tennis competitions twice a year. So does Joan"
                - Past Simple
                - the auxiliary verb is did
                - "Last week we qualified for the finals. So did we"
                - structure of the short comment 
                - Really?/Oh + subject (noun/pronoun) + auxiliary/modal verb in form opposite to used one in statement to which we are reacting
                - I believe that some children should start training at a very early age. Really? I do not
                - Most of our fans could not get tickets to watch the match. Oh. Ours could

Writting
<p>
                <!---->
                story <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - Beginning
                - use an opening sentence and interesting title that makes the reader want to read on
                - set the scene for the story so the reader can imagine what might happen next
                - finish the beginning section with a problem, or at point which is exciting or interesting
                - Telling your story
                - use a range of different narrative tenses to tell the story- Use the Past Continuous to set the scene
                - We were both feeling quite nervous
                - use past simple to describe the main events
                - We took the lift up the mountain
                - use the past perfect to make it clear that one past action happened before another past action
                - We had booked lessons before we arrived
                - use adverbs and strong adjectives to make the story exciting
                - By the end of the first day, we were completely exhausted
                - use sequencers so the reader can follow the story
                - twenty minutes later the three of us were in a helicopter ...
                - use one or two short sentences for the dramatic effect
                - we discovered Max at the bottom of a big hole
                - use some direct speech to make the story come alive
                - "Where did he go?" I asked Lilly.
                - Ending your story
                - Think of an exciting, funny or unexpected ending to the story to help the reader remember it
              </p>
