
● Grammar
● First Conditional
● Used to talk about the possible results of an action
● Sentences refer to the future
○ Example: "If I tell them the truth, they will not believe me."
● To form First Conditional, we have 2 choices
● Formation A: condition (If + Present Simple) + comma (,) + result (will (not) + infinitive)
○ Example: "If he works hard, he will pass his exams."
● Formation B: result (will (not) + infinitive) + condition (If + Present Simple)
○ Example: "He will pass his exams if he works hard."
○ (SLIKA 1)
● Defining relative clauses
● We define them to determine essential information about a person, thing or place following relative pronouns
● who and that
● Used to talk about people
○ Example: "This is the teacher who/that teaches my class."
● which and that
● Used to talk about things
○ Example: "Is this the laptop which/that you ordered?"
● where
● Used to talk about places
○ Example: "Ella went to a school where most pupils were girls."
● All of these relative pronouns usually come after the noun (person/thing/place) they refer to

● Language
● Future time and conditional clauses
● Conditional clauses
● Introduced with these examples
○ if
○ Example: "If Mark does not hand in his homework today, his teacher will get really angry."
○ unless
○ Example: "Amy will not make any progress unless she works systematically."
● Time clauses
● Introduced with these examples
○ when
○ Example: "When the new term starts, the teachers will meet up."
○ before
○ Example: "Before I take Chemistry and Physics for my A levels, I will talk to my teachers."
○ after
○ Example: "After you mark your homework, you will see your mistakes."
○ as soon as
○ Example: "The kids will feel better as soon as the school breaks up for holiday."
● Present Simple is used in future time and conditional clauses
● Formation: Subordinate clause (if/unless/when/before/after/as soon as + Present Simple) + Main clause (will (not) + verb)
○ Example: "My sister will never succeed unless she learns from her mistakes."
● Either clause may come first in the sentence
● We put a comma at the end of the subordinate clause if it comes first in the sentence
○ Example: "As soon as I get my new timetable, I will let you know about the time of our meeting."
● We do not put a comma at the end of the main clause if it comes first in the sentence
○ Example: "I will let you know about the time of our meeting as soon as I get my new timetable."
○ (SLIKA 1)
● Indirect questions
● Used in formal letters and emails to be more formal
● Formation: subject + verb (+object)
○ Example: "How long will I need to study?" = "Could you tell me how long I will need to study etc."
● When there is no question word in a direct question, you use if or whether in the indirect question
○ Example: "Can you arrange accommodation for me?" = "Could you tell me if you could arrange accommodation for me etc."

● Writing
● An enquiry
● Start the email with Dear Mr or Mrs and the person's surname (if you do not know the person's surname, use Sir or Madam)
● Instead of abbreviations or contractions, use full forms
○ Example: "I would etc." (not "I'd")
● Use formal expressions to:
● say why you are writing the email
○ "I am writing to etc."
● ask for information
○ "I would also like to know if etc."
● ask somebody to do something for you
○ "I would be grateful if etc."
● say that you expect a reply
○ "I look forward to etc."
● Finish the email with "Yours sincerely"
