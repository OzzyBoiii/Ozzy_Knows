
● Present and past habits
  ○ To talk about someone’s habits and typical behaviour, we can use:
    ● Present Simple or Past Simple for repeated actions or states
      ○ When I get home, I do my homework and practise the violin.
      ○ My grandfather picked me up from school every day.
    ● used to + infinitive for past states or actions no longer happening
      ○ When I was at primary school, I used to be very eager to learn.
      ○ I didn’t use to fall behind with my schoolwork.
      ○ Did you use to like Chemistry?
      ○ Not used for single past actions.
    ● would + infinitive for typical past actions (not states)
      ○ Our Maths teacher would give us loads of homework.
    ● will + infinitive for repeated present actions or typical behaviour (not states)
      ○ She’ll watch TV or play games all day instead of studying for her exams.
      ○ Note: Not used in questions.
    ● Present Continuous or Past Continuous with always to complain
      ○ She’s always putting things off.
      ○ My neighbours were always complaining about the noise.

● Verb patterns
  ○ Verbs followed by other verbs may follow these patterns:
    ● verb + to + infinitive
      ○ agree, arrange, can’t afford, decide, intend, learn, manage, offer, prefer, refuse, remember, seem, tend
      ○ I decided to leave school and find a job.
      ○ My sister agreed to help me with my homework.
    ● verb + object + to + infinitive
      ○ advise, allow, cause, enable, encourage, expect, force, remind, urge, warn
      ○ Robert’s mother encouraged him to study Law.
      ○ The teacher reminded us not to use our mobile phones during lessons.
    ● verb + object + infinitive without to
      ○ make, let
      ○ Do his parents let him hang out with his friends?
      ○ My parents used to make me do a lot of house chores.
    ● verb + gerund (-ing form)
      ○ admit, avoid, can’t help, can’t stand, enjoy, fancy, imagine, keep, miss, practise, spend/waste time, stop
      ○ I can’t stand wearing a school uniform.
      ○ She recently stopped eating fast food, and she feels better already.
    ● verb + gerund / to + infinitive (same or slight change)
      ○ begin, continue, hate, intend, like, love, prefer, start
      ○ I started playing chess.
      ○ I started to play chess.
      ○ He intends using the school website.
      ○ He intends to use the school website.
    ● Change in meaning with remember, forget, stop, try
      ○ Jenny remembers locking the door. (She remembers that she locked the door.)
      ○ Jenny remembers to lock the door. (She always remembers to lock the door.)
      ○ Dad’ll forget giving me the money. (He will forget that he gave me the money.)
      ○ Dad’ll forget to give me the money. (He hasn’t given me the money and he’ll forget to do it.)
      ○ Liz stopped drinking coffee. (Liz doesn’t drink coffee anymore.)
      ○ Liz stopped to drink some coffee. (Liz stopped doing something in order to drink coffee.)
      ○ I tried turning the computer off and on again, but it didn’t help. (experiment)
      ○ I tried to turn the computer off, but the shutdown command didn’t work. (effort)
    ● feel, hear, see, watch + infinitive without to (whole action)
      ○ I saw Mary paint a picture. (from start to finish)
    ● feel, hear, see, watch + -ing form (part of action)
      ○ I saw Mary painting a picture. (in progress)

● Register: formal and informal language
  ○ Formal language is used in official documents, letters, emails to unknown people, official invitations, notifications, presentations, speeches.
  ○ Informal language is used in private conversations, letters/emails to friends, notes, postcards.
  ○ Grammar differences:
    ● Full verb forms in formal; contractions in informal
      ○ I am contacting you ... / I’m contacting you …
    ● No abbreviations in formal; abbreviations used in informal
      ○ Could you do it as soon as possible? / Could you do it ASAP?
    ● More passive voice and indirect speech in formal; active and direct in informal
      ○ The dissertation must be submitted today. / You must submit the dissertation today.
      ○ It is said. / They say that spinach is healthy.
    ● Indirect questions in formal (I was wondering...); direct in informal
      ○ I was wondering if there are enough volunteers. / Are there enough volunteers?
  ○ Vocabulary differences:
    ● Complex expressions and abstract nouns in formal; simpler in informal
      ○ employment opportunities / a chance to get a job
      ○ Is it possible to be happy? / Is happiness possible?
    ● Words of Latin origin in formal (to depart, to cease); Anglo-Saxon in informal (to go, to stop)
    ● Common verbs in formal (appear); phrasal verbs in informal (turn up)
    ● Complex prepositional phrases in formal (in favour of); simpler in informal (for)
    ● Formal linkers: furthermore, however; informal: and, but

● Use these phrases to give concise information on your CV (avoid full sentences)
  ○ competent user of Microsoft Office
  ○ dedicated member of care team
  ○ excellent command of Danish and Spanish
  ○ practical experience of caring for someone
  ○ proven ability to work well in team
  ○ well-developed

● Use these phrases to explain more about your work experience in a covering email (use full sentences)
  ○ I gained considerable work experience.
  ○ I worked alongside teachers.
  ○ I attended but didn't take part in classes.
  ○ I supervised the children at break times.
  ○ I sat in on staff meetings.
  ○ I coordinated a sports day.

● Organising a CV
  ○ One to two pages long.
  ○ Save space and make concise by avoiding full sentences.
  ○ Use headings and bullet points for logical organisation.
  ○ Use positive language to describe skills and experience.
  ○ Choose referees linked to education and work experience.
  ○ Check if a photo is required in the country of application.

===================================================

● Organising a covering email
  ○ Use full sentences and a variety of positive language.
  ○ Say why you are writing and which job you are applying for.
  ○ Give details about yourself and explain why you want the job.
  ○ Explain more about relevant skills and experience (don't just repeat CV).
  ○ Mention availability for interview and any attachments (e.g., CV).

● Describing a photo
  ○ Speculating about people
    ● Based on ..., I'd say he's/she's/it's...
    ● Judging by..., I (don't) think...
    ● It looks/seems as if/as though...
    ● He/She/It appears/doesn't appear to be...
    ● The chances are (that) he's/she's/it's...
  ○ Showing certainty
    ● Clearly, (there's a problem/something is wrong).
    ● It's obvious/clear (from the expression on his/her face) that...
    ● He/She/It is definitely/certainly (not)...
  ○ Showing uncertainty
    ● It's hard to be sure (whether/if...)
    ● It's not easy to say (whether/if...)
    ● I can't really tell (whether/if...)
  ○ Speculating about the situation
    ● He/She could/might have ('ve) just + Past Participle (recent past)
    ● He/She could/might be + -ing (present)
    ● He/She could/might be about to + infinitive (near future)
