
● Dynamic and state verbs (OVERVIEWED)
  ○ Dynamic verbs (do, work, play)
    ● describe actions
    ● they can be used in both types of tenses (Present Simple and Present Continuous)
    ● "I often listen to classical music."
    ● "I am listening to Mozart now."
  ○ state verbs
    ● mostly used in Present Simple tenses, even if they refer to something happening at the moment of speaking
    ● "Do you understand me?" (state)
    ● "What are you looking at?" (action)
    ● sometimes used in the Present Continuous, but with the change of meaning (think, have, look)
    ● "Do I look good in this dress?" (state)
    ● "What are you looking at?" (action)
    ● they include
      ○ attitude verbs (describing emotions (hate, like, love, prefer))
      ○ mental/thinking verbs (believe, know, need, remember, think, understand, want)
      ○ sense/perception verbs (feel, hear, see)

● Present perfect Continuous (OVERVIEWED)
  ○ used to talk about
    ● actions which started in the past and continue into the present
    ● "I have been waiting for her since 8 o'clock"
    ● events which lasted for some time (and may continue into the present) and whose results can be seen now
    ● "I am tired because I have been painting all day"
    ● common type expressions: for, since
  ○ usage
    ● used to focus on an action or process (which may or may not be complete)
    ● its questions often begin with How long
    ● "How long has she been writing books?"
    ● It is not used when in questions in either of the two tenses
  ○ formation
    ● Affirmative sentence
      ○ I/you/we/they + have + been crying.
      ○ he/she/it + has + been crying.
    ● Negative sentence
      ○ I/you/we/they + have not + been crying.
      ○ he/she/it + has not + been crying.
    ● Yes/No questions
      ○ Have + I/you/we/they + been watching TV? Yes, I/you/we/they have/No, I/you/we/they have not
      ○ Has + he/she/it + been crying? Yes, he/she/it has/No, he/she/it has not
    ● Wh- questions
      ○ How long + have + I/you/we/they + been crying?
      ○ How long + has + he/she/it + been crying?
    ● Subject questions
      ○ Who + has + been crying?

● Common suffixes (OVERVIEWED)
  ○ used to form different words by adding them to nouns, verbs and adjectives
  ○ Most common suffixes
    ● used to form nouns
      ○ -ance/-ence - used to make nouns from adjectives by changing the -ant/-ent ending - important - importance
      ○ -ion - used to form nouns from verbs - decide - decision
      ○ -ity - stupid - stupidity
      ○ -ment - develop - development
      ○ -ship - relation - relationship
    ● used to form verbs
      ○ -ate (local - locate)
      ○ -en (usually added to adjectives and nouns related to measurement)
      ○ -ify (simple - simplify)
      ○ -ise (legal - legalise)
    ● used to form adjectives
      ○ -able (comfort - comfortable)
      ○ -ed (amaze - amazed)
      ○ -ing (amaze - amazing)
      ○ -ful (colour - colourful)
      ○ -less (colour - colourless)
      ○ -ive (support - supportive)
      ○ -ous (courage - courageous)
      ○ -ic (energy - energetic)
      ○ -al (economy - economical)
    ● used to form adverbs
      ○ -ly (strange - strangely)
      ○ -y (used when adjective ends with -le (possible - possibly))
      ○ -ily (used when adjective ends with -y (heavy - heavily))
    ● used for some adjectives to get the opposite meaning
      ○ -un (familiar - unfamiliar)
      ○ -im (most often added to adjectives beginning with p, b and m - balanced - imbalanced - mature - immature - polite - impolite)
      ○ -ir (most often added to adjectives beginning with r (responsible - irresponsible))
      ○ -il (most often added to adjectives beginning with l (legal - illegal))
      ○ -dis (respectful - disrespectful)
      ○ -in (sensitive - insensitive)

● Tentative language
  ○ makes language less negative or extreme
  ○ Examples
    ● kind of/sort of
      ○ She looks kind of cool/unusual/nervous
    ● tend to be/get + adjective
      ○ She gets upset = She tends to be upset
    ● can be/could be + a little/a bit + adjective
      ○ His hair is too long = His hair could be a bit shorter
      ○ She is moody = She can be a bit emotional
    ● negative adjective to positive adjective with always, exactly, particularly
      ○ He is lazy = He is not always hard-working
      ○ He is mean = He is not particularly generous.
      ○ She is always late = She is not always on time
    ● Quantifiers/softeners
      ○ She is skinny = She is a bit too slim
      ○ He is tiny = He is a little short
      ○ She is old = She looks old

● Tentative language: making language less negative or extreme
  ○ kind of / sort of
    ● She looks kind of cool / unusual / mysterious / nervous / (1) punky.
  ○ tend to be / get + adjective
    ● She gets upset. = She tends (2) .
  ○ can be / could be + a little / a bit + adjective
    ● His hair’s too long. = His hair could be a bit shorter.
    ● She’s moody. = She can be (3) .
  ○ Negative adjective to positive adjective with always, exactly, particularly
    ● He’s lazy. = He isn’t always hard-working.
    ● He’s mean. = He’s not particularly generous.
    ● She’s always late. = She isn’t (4) .
  ○ Quantifiers / softeners
    ● She’s skinny. = She’s a bit too slim.
    ● He’s tiny. = He’s a little short.
    ● She’s old. = She looks (5) .

● Describing a person (OVERVIEWED)
  ○ Give first impressions
    ● The first thing you notice about her is that she looks older than she is
  ○ Mention age
    ● He is (about) my/our/your age
    ● He is in his teens/early/mid-/late twenties
  ○ Describe personality and interests
    ● He is a very easy-going/interesting etc. person
    ● She has a great sense of humour
    ● She is the sort/type/kind of person who always remembers your birthday/loves kids
    ● She would make a great teacher/doctor/friend
    ● He is into music/fashion/skateboarding
  ○ Describe hair, eyes, skin and face
    ● He has got cool, short, dark hair
    ● She has got beautiful, long, straight, blond hair
    ● She has got a kind/friendly/unusual smile/face
  ○ Mention height/build
    ● she is short/medium height/tall
    ● He is fairly well-built/slim
  ○ Mention clothes
    ● She dresses casually/smartly/well/in black
    ● He always wears casual/smart/scruffy/fashionable/stylish clothes

● Describing a photo (OVERVIEWED)
  ○ Beginning a description
    ● The photo shows ...
    ● In this photo, I can see .../there is .../there are ...
  ○ Saying where
    ● In the background/in the middle/in the foreground
    ● on the left/on the right
    ● In front of/behind/next to
  ○ Showing uncertainty
    ● It is hard to say/make out ..., but ...
    ● I am not sure ..., but ...
  ○ Speculating
    ● He/she/it looks ...
    ● He/she/it looks as if/as though/like ...
    ● It seems to be .../Perhaps it is .../Maybe it is ...
    ● I imagine they are/ they are probably ...
  ○ Giving your opinion
    ● I (do not) think.../I prefer .../Personally, .../In my opinion ...
