● Adjectives:
○ one-syllable: young → younger (than) → the youngest
○ one- and two-syllable ending in -y: pretty → prettier (than) → the prettiest
○ two-syllable or longer: attractive → more attractive (than) → the most attractive
○ Irregular: good → better (than) → the best

● Present Perfect with just, already, (not) yet and Past Simple (OVERVIEWED)
○ Present Perfect
● its usage
○ used to talk about actions which happened and finished in the past, but we do not know when exactly
● "I have read a lot of biographies and autobiographies."
○ used to talk about actions which happened in the past and the result is visible in the present
● "Eddie has painted his first potrait."
○ Common time expressions used with Present Perfect
● expression ever is used in questions
○ "Have you ever been to an art gallery?"
● expression never is used in negative sentences
○ "My grandparents have never left England."
● expression since then
○ "She won The X Factor in 2006. Since then she has sold millions of albums."
● expressions already and just are used mainly in affirmative sentences between have and the Past Participle verb form
○ "I have already seen it."
○ "They have just left."
● expression yet is used in negative sentences and questions and always at the end of the sentence
○ "I have not seen her yet."
● time expression Past Simple is used if we want to say when something happened or with questions with when
○ "She won a Grammy in 2016."
○ "When did you see Blur play live?"

● too and not enough (OVERVIEWED)
○ both are used to make comparisons and talk about a degree of a quality
○ too
● means "more than you need or want"
● its usage
○ before adjectives
● "The painting is too dark i cannot see what is in it."
○ with nouns with expressions to many/much
● "There are too many horror films at the cinema these days."
○ in an affirmative sentence (to create negative meaning)
● "The plot is too complex." (I cannot understand it)
○ with negation (to create positive meaning)
● "The plot is not too complex." (It is easy to understand)
○ (not) enough
● enough means "the amount you need"
○ "We had enough photos to prepare."
● not enough means "less than you need or want"
○ "We did not have enough photos to prepare."
● its usage
○ after adjectives
● "The special effects were not realistic enough."
○ before nouns
● "There are enough cooking programmes on TV nowadays."

● Adjectives to describe films, plots, screenplays etc. (OVERVIEWED)
○ We use
● superb, brilliant, inspiring to mean fantastic
● engaging to mean something pleasant
● amusing to mean funny
● thrilling to mean something that makes you excited and makes you want to do something important
● convincing to mean something which makes you believe something is right
● predictable to mean something not surprising or you expected to happen
