Grammar
○ Past Continuous and Past Simple (OVERVIEWED)
○ Past Continuous
○ used to describe a longer background scene in a story, during which other main events (described in the Past Simple) took place
○ "Someone was sitting at desk when the lights went out."
○ used to talk about a longer action (Past Continuous) that was interrupted by the shorter action (Past Simple)
○ "While Meg was texting a message to her friend, she dropped her phone."
○ used to talk about actions in progress at the same time
○ "While Ann was doing some experiments, Terry was taking some measurements."
○ formation
○ Affirmative sentence
○ I/he/she/it + was + watching TV.
○ you/we/they + were + watching TV.
○ Negative sentence
○ I/he/she/it + was not + watching TV.
○ you/we/they + were not + watching TV.
○ Yes/No questions
○ Was + I/he/she/it + watching TV? Yes, I/he/she/it was/No, I/he/she/it was not
○ Were + you/we/they + watching TV? Yes, you/we/they were/No, you/we/they were not
○ Wh- questions
○ What + was + I/he/she/it + watching?
○ What + were + you/we/they + watching?
○ Subject questions
○ Who + was + watching TV?
○ (SLIKA 1)
○ Used to (OVERVIEWED)
○ the usage of used to
○ past states or actions that are no longer true
○ "I used to play tennis a lot." (I do not play either very often or anymore)
○ When we talk about single actions that happened once, we use Past Simple (not used to)
○ Kim bought a new mobile phone yesterday.
○ formation
○ Affirmative sentence
○ I/you/he/she/it/we/they + used to + swim.
○ Negative sentence
○ I/you/he/she/it/we/they + did not use to + swim.
○ Yes/No questions
○ Did + I/you/he/she/it/we/they + use to + swim? Yes, I/you/he/she/it/we/they did/No, I/you/he/she/it/we/they did not
○ Wh- questions
○ Where + did + I/you/he/she/it/we/they + use to + swim?
○ Subject questions
○ Who + used to + swim?
○ (SLIKA 2)

● Language
○ Linkers and time expressions (OVERVIEWED)
○ are followed by full sentences (i.e., linker + subject + main verb).
○ While and when are used to link two sentences describing events taking place at the same time:
○ While the astronauts were collecting specimens on the Moon, they found some interesting rocks.
○ David became interested in psychology when he was in high school.
○ After, as soon as, before and when are used to link two sentences describing events taking place one after another:
○ After Mark spent hours observing the work of archaeologists, he realised how important their job is.
○ I checked the price of the new smartphone before I bought it.
○ As soon as Ann published the photos of her new experiments on social media, she got a lot of positive comments.
○ When Sarah received text messages asking for the password to her account, she deleted them straight away.
○ Linkers and time expressions are followed by nouns or noun phrases:
○ during the holidays, for twenty-two days, until late evening, by the end of the century.
○ During refers to a period of time:
○ The linguist explored several languages during his stay in India.
○ Until and till refer to a point in time:
○ We didn't do many experiments in Chemistry classes until (till) last year.
○ By, when used with a noun, refers to an event which is happening or which happened before a particular point in time:
○ By the time we got home, we were tired and hungry.
○ in narrative stories, these linkers are used (OVERVIEWED)
○ I have to say or I must admit to emphasise the next piece of information
○ Anyhow or anyway to change the subject or move the story on
○ Actually or In fact to show that the next piece of information is surprising
○ And, but, because, so to join parts of sentences
○ Then, when, while, during to make the order of events clear

● Writing
○ A story
○ set the scene
○ I was twelve years old when ...
○ It was late in the evening when ...
○ I was in the classroom when ...
○ Use questions, short dramatic sentences and occasional exclamation marks to show surprise or excitement
○ What was going on?
○ What a surprise!
○ It was awesome!
○ We were in trouble.
○ Use adverbs to add interest
○ All of a sudden/suddenly/Luckily/Unfortunately
○ ... really (good)
○ ... completely (lost)
○ ... incredibly (smart)
○ Use a summarising statement to begin or end your story
○ I will never forget the day I first saw .../the time I decided to .../when I first went .../... was an event I will never forget

● Speaking
○ Telling a story
○ Use the right tenses
○ Past Continuous for the "background"
○ "The sun was shining and I was enjoying myself."
○ Past Simple for the "problem" and the "main events"
○ "The weather changed and I could not see the path."
○ Use linkers
○ Beginning: To start with/At first
○ middle: Suddenly/All of a sudden/Luckily/Fortunately/Unfortunately
○ End: In the end/Finally/Eventually
○ Say how you felt
○ I was excited/frightened/surprised/shocked/worried, etc.
○ Make a "final comment"
○ "It was the best/worst day of my life!"
○ "I will never do it again."
○ Listening to a story
○ Neutral response: Really?/Oh dear./Oh no
○ Strong response:
○ That sounds amazing/funny/frightening.
○ What a great story/a nightmare!
○ Respond with questions
○ What happened?
○ What did you do?
