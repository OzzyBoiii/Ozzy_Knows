Grammar
<p>
                <!---->
                Present Perfect with for and since <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - used to talk about unfinished situations that started in the past and continue in time "up to now" <br>
                - expressions = how long, since, for <br>
                - you use expression how long to ask about the length of time "up to now" <br>
                - "How long until you work for CS" <br>
                - you use expression since when the answer is a point in time <br>
                - "I was here since it started in 2004" <br>
                - you use expression for when the answer is a period of time <br>
                - "I worked with people from all over the world for more than 10 years." <br>
                - questions are formed using How long ... <br>
                - "How long have you lived in this house? <br>
                <!---->
                Future forms (Present Continuous, be going to and will) <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - Present Continuous 
                - used for future arrangements which have been already been planned and prepared 
                - you often mention a time, a date, or a place 
                - "I am throwing a birthday party on the 25th." 
                - Be going to + infinitive 
                - used for future intentions which may still be changed 
                - you have already decided to do something and you tell people about it 
                - formation 
                - Affirmative sentence 
                - I + am + going to sleep 
                - he/she/it + is + going to sleep 
                - you/we/they + are + going to sleep 
                - Negative sentence 
                - I + am not + going to sleep 
                - he/she/it + is not + going to sleep 
                - you/we/they + are not + going to sleep 
                - Yes/No questions 
                - Am + I + going to sleep Yes, I am/No, I am not 
                - Is + he/she/it + going to sleep? Yes, he/she/it is/No, he/she/it is not 
                - Are + you/we/they + going to sleep? Yes, you/we/they are/No, you/we/they are not 
                - Wh- questions - When + am + I + going to sleep? 
                - When + is + he/she/it + going to sleep? 
                - When + are + you/we/they + going to sleep? 
                - Subject questions 
                - Who + is + going to sleep? 
                - will + infinitive 
                - used for spontaneous decisions made at the moment of speaking 
                - you often use expressions like "I think i WIll..." or "I will probably..." or "Don"t worry, I will..." 
                - formation 
                - Affirmative sentence 
                - I + will + go to sleep 
                - he/she/it + will + go to sleep 
                - you/we/they + will + go to sleep 
                - Negative sentence 
                - I + will not + go to sleep 
                - he/she/it + will not + go to sleep 
                - you/we/they + will not + go to sleep 
                - Yes/No questions 
                - Will + I + go to sleep Yes, I will/No, I will not 
                - Will + he/she/it + go to sleep? Yes, he/she/it will/No, he/she/it will not 
                - Will + you/we/they + go to sleep? Yes, you/we/they will/No, you/we/they will not 
                - Wh- questions 
                - When + will + I + go to sleep? 
                - When + will + he/she/it + go to sleep? 
                - When + will + you/we/they + go to sleep? 
                - Subject questions - Who + will + go to sleep?

###TEXT2###
Language
<p>
                <!---->
                Adverbs <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - used to modify and define verbs, adjectives and other adverbs 
                - adverbs with verbs ("Are you sitting comfortably?") 
                - adverbs with adjectives ("The living room is really beautiful.") 
                - adverbs with other adverbs ("We drove incredibly slowly because of the traffic jams.") 
                - their formation - adjective + -ly (slow - slowly) 
                - adjective + -y, (unbelievable - unbelievably) 
                - adjective + -ily (lucky - luckily) 
                - some adverbs and adjectives have the same spelling (hard, fast, late, early) 
                - adverb is never put between a verb and its object 
                - "He speaks well English." 
                - Comparasion 
                - you form comparative with more 
                - "Alice speaks more clearly than John." 
                - some of the adverbs are irregular - well - better - hard - harder 
                - you form superlative with the most 
                - "Alice speaks the most clearly." 
                - adverbs of degree 
                - used before both adjectives and adverbs to modify their meaning 
                - a little/a bit/slightly 
                - "Sue says that living in the suburbs is slightly better now." 
                - quite/rather/pretty 
                - "Since we redecorated the room, it looks pretty good." 
                - really/extremely/completely 
                - "You need to move it extremely carefully because it is an antiue." 
                <!---->
                Punctuation - commas <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - usage of commas 
                - used between the names of cities and states or countries 
                - used to separate three or more nouns in a list 
                - used to seperate clauses in a sentence when they are joined by but or so 
                - used to seperate and if-clause from the main clause when the if-clause is the first one in a sentence 
                - used after time expressions at the beginning of a sentence
              </p>
