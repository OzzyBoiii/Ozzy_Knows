                Second Conditional<br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - its usage
                - used to talk about the imaginary situations in the present 
                - "If I were rich, I would live in a huge house."
                - used to talk about improbable events in the future
                - "If he left home earlier, he would never be late for work."
                - Past Simple is used in the clause to describe the result in the if-clause and would/would not
                - To form Second Conditional, we have 2 choices
                - Formation A: condition (If + Past Simple) + result (would (not) + infinitive)
                - "If Sue knew Italian, she would apply for this job."
                - Formation B: result (would (not) + infinitive) + condition (If + Past Simple) 
                - "Sue would apply for this job if she knew Italian."

                - do not need to/need not

                <!---->
                Adjectives ending in -ed and -ing <br>(OVERVIEWED)
                - -ing adjectives
                - used to describe people, objects/places and events
                - people ("A growing child needs to eat a lot.")
                - objects/places ("My new office is rather depressing because it is very small and dark.")
                - events ("The first job interview is usually challenging.")
                - -ed adjectives
                - they are used to describe states (of people or objects) and emotions/feelings
                - states ("The instructor was a determined person.")
                - emotions/feelings ("Maria looked rather confused when she first arrived in our office.")
                - Pairs of -ed/-ing adjectives
                - formed from the same verb
                - amuse = amusing = amused
                - Some -ed adjectives do not have an -ing equivalent
                - delight = delighted
