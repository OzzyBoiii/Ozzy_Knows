Grammar
<p>
                <!---->
                Present and past speculation <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - speculating about the present
                - when speculating about a present situation, we use a modal verb+infinitive. We use
                - must
                - express a strong belief that something is true
                - John must be very happy working at the zoo
                - might, may, could
                - when we think that it's possible that something is true
                - The lions might/may/could be very hungry now.
                - can't
                - express  a strong belief that something isn't true
                - It can't be a domestic cat. It's too big
                - speculating about the past
                - when speculating about a past situation or event, we use a modal verb+have+past participle form of main verb. We use
                - must have
                - express a strong belief that something happened
                - You must have lost your mobile t the zoo
                - might have, may have
                - when we think that it's possible that something happened
                - Lucy might/may/could have missed the train
                - can't have, couldn't have
                - express a strong belief that something didn't happen
                - Peter can't/couldn't have gone home
                <!---->
                Used to and would (+infinitive) <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - talk about past states or actions that happened regularly in the past
                - used to
                - used for states () or actions
                - I didn't use to like travelling by plane (but I do now)
                - She used to write travel guides (But she doesn't any more)
                - would
                - used for actions (but not states) in affirmative sentences
                - When I was a teenager, I would visit my grandma every Sunday

Language
<p>
                <!---->
                Phrasal verbs- verb+particle(s) <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - expressions that consist of a verb or a particle, thanks to which the verb gains a new meaning
                - look- look up (=check)
                - most phrasal verbs have their equivalents in formal language
                - put up a tent = construct/erect a tent
                meaning of phrasal verbs
                - literal (Why don't you take off your coat?)
                - idiomatic (The plane takes off at 10 a.m.)
                - division of phrasal verbs
                - inseparable
                -  verb + particle + object
                - There is always an object after the phrasal verb
                - When I was running, I bumped into my form teacher
                - We dropped our bags and made for the passport control
                -  verb + particle
                - There is no object after the phrasal verbs
                - Fortunately, a lot of people turned up to the exhibition
                - It was a long journey so we set off early in the morning
                - verb + particle + particle + object
                - there is always an object after the phrasal verb
                - I look foward to travelling around America next summer
                - It was very hot and we ran out of water very quickly
                - separable
                - there is always an object (If the object is a noun, it can come before or after the particle)
                - types
                - verb + particle + object
                - Can you please pick up Laura from the airport
                - Can you please pick up her from the airport
                - verb + object + particle
                - Can you please pick Laura up from the airport?
                - Can you please pick her up from the airport?
                <!---->
                Ellipsis <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - used in informal English for leaving the words out
                - usually leave out subject pronouns and auxiliary verbs at the beginning of a clause when the meaning is obvious
                - Having a great time. - I am having a great time...
                - Seems like five minutes ago ...= It seems like five minutes ago
                - you can also leave out repeated words
                - Feeling a bit tired but I always am. - Im feeling a bit tired but I'm always tired

                Ellipsis
                In informal English, you can leave words out. This is called ellipsis. You usually leave out subject pronouns and auxiliary verbs at the beginning of a clause when the meaning is obvious: 
                Having a great time … = (1) 
                having a great time …
                Can’t believe you’re going to uni … = (2)  can’t believe you’re going to uni … 
                Seems like five minutes ago … = (3)  seems like five minutes ago …
                Great to have a DJ for a little brother. = (4)  great to have a DJ for a little brother. 
                Hope to see you soon. = (5) 
                hope to see you soon. 
                You can also leave out repeated words:
                Feeling a bit tired but I always am. = I’m feeling a bit tired but I’m always tired.

Writting
<p>
                <!---->
                A peronal email giving advice <br> (OVERVIEWED)
                - start with general news and/or a reference to what your friend wrote in their last email
                - express sympathy for your friend's situation
                - I understand what a difficult decision it is
                - I can see why you're worried/unhappy
                - offer advice by asking a question
                - Have you thought about ...
                - Why don't you ...
                - offer advice by making a statement
                - If I were you, I would ...
                - It's a good idea to ...
                - Change the subject and say something positive or give more news
                - By the way, thanks for ...
                - Incidentally, did you hear that ...?
                - Reassure your friend at the end of the email
                - I'm sure everything will be ok
                - I'm sure things will work out fine
              </p>

● Spekulacija o sadašnjosti i prošlosti

    ○ Spekulacija o sadašnjosti
        ○ Kada nagađamo o sadašnjoj situaciji, koristimo modalni glagol + infinitiv
    ○ must
        ○ Izražava jako uvjerenje da je nešto istinito
        ○ ● John must be very happy working at the zoo
    ○ might, may, could
        ○ Koristimo kada mislimo da je moguće da je nešto istinito
        ○ ● The lions might be very hungry now
        ○ ● The lions may be very hungry now
        ○ ● The lions could be very hungry now
    ○ can't
        ○ Izražava jako uvjerenje da nešto nije istinito
        ○ ● It can't be a domestic cat
        ○ ● It's too big

    ○ Spekulacija o prošlosti
        ○ Kada nagađamo o prošloj situaciji ili događaju, koristimo modalni glagol + have + past participle glavnog glagola
    ○ must have
        ○ Izražava jako uvjerenje da se nešto dogodilo
        ○ ● You must have lost your mobile at the zoo
    ○ might have, may have
        ○ Kada mislimo da je moguće da se nešto dogodilo
        ○ ● Lucy might have missed the train
        ○ ● Lucy may have missed the train
        ○ ● Lucy could have missed the train
    ○ can't have, couldn't have
        ○ Izražava jako uvjerenje da se nešto nije dogodilo
        ○ ● Peter can't have gone home
        ○ ● Peter couldn't have gone home

● Used to i would (+ infinitiv)

    ○ Koristimo za govor o prošlim stanjima ili radnjama koje su se redovito događale u prošlosti
    ○ used to
        ○ Koristi se za stanja ili radnje
        ○ ● I didn't use to like travelling by plane
        ○ ● (ali sada volim)
        ○ ● She used to write travel guides
        ○ ● (Ali više ne piše)
    ○ would
        ○ Koristi se za radnje (ali ne za stanja) u potvrdnim rečenicama
        ○ ● When I was a teenager, I would visit my grandma every Sunday

    ○ (TABLICA 1)
        ○ Potvrdni oblik: I used to go backpacking every summer / I would go backpacking every summer
        ○ Niječni oblik: I didn't use to buy flight tickets online
        ○ Da/ne pitanje: Did he use to go on holiday in August?
        ○ Pitanja s upitnim riječima: What did he use to eat?
        ○ Pitanja s upitnom riječju kao subjektom: Who used to go to the seaside on holiday?

● Frazealni glagoli (glagol + čestica/e)

    ○ Izrazi koji se sastoje od glagola i čestice, zahvaljujući kojima glagol dobiva novo značenje
    ○ ● look - look up (= provjeriti)
    ○ Većina frazealnih glagola ima svoje ekvivalente u formalnom jeziku
    ○ ● put up a tent = construct/erect a tent

    ○ Značenje frazealnih glagola
        ○ Doslovno
            ○ ● Why don't you take off your coat?
        ○ Idiomatsko
            ○ ● The plane takes off at 10 a.m.

    ○ Podjela frazealnih glagola
        ○ Nerazdvojni
            ○ glagol + čestica + objekt
                ○ Uvijek postoji objekt nakon frazealnog glagola
                ○ ● When I was running, I bumped into my form teacher
                ○ ● We dropped our bags and made for the passport control
            ○ glagol + čestica
                ○ Nema objekta nakon frazealnog glagola
                ○ ● Fortunately, a lot of people turned up to the exhibition
                ○ ● It was a long journey so we set off early in the morning
            ○ glagol + čestica + čestica + objekt
                ○ Uvijek postoji objekt nakon frazealnog glagola
                ○ ● I look forward to travelling around America next summer
                ○ ● It was very hot and we ran out of water very quickly
        ○ Razdvojni
            ○ Uvijek postoji objekt
            ○ Ako je objekt imenica, može doći prije ili poslije čestice
            ○ glagol + čestica + objekt
                ○ ● Can you please pick up Laura from the airport?
                ○ ● (Can you please pick up her from the airport - notirano kao primjer netočnog)
            ○ glagol + objekt + čestica
                ○ ● Can you please pick Laura up from the airport?
                ○ ● Can you please pick her up from the airport?

● Elipsa

    ○ Koristi se u neformalnom engleskom za izostavljanje riječi
    ○ Obično se izostavljaju subjektne zamjenice i pomoćni glagoli na početku rečenice kada je značenje očito
        ○ ● Having a great time
        ○ ● (I am having a great time)
        ○ ● Seems like five minutes ago
        ○ ● (It seems like five minutes ago)
    ○ Također se mogu izostaviti ponovljene riječi
        ○ ● Feeling a bit tired but I always am
