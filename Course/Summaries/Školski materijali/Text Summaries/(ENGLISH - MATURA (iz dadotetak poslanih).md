What is the aim of reading tasks?
Their aim is to improve your skills in the techniques of:
Skimming 
        - we read the text quickly to get the general idea of what the text is about without stopping at unknown words
         Faster reading aids comprehension.
Scanning 
	- we go through the text quickly looking for specific pieces of information, or to locate the part of the text where the answer is
Close/intensive reading
	- involves reading and trying to understand every word
	- it might involve reading something more than once

What is a For-and-against essay?Also known as „argumentative” essays, these essays are
used to report on the positive and negative sides of a
particular topic.
 Unlike opinion essays, argumentative essays have a strict
FORMAT, CONTENT and STYLE which have to be followed
throughout the entire essay
For-and-against Essay Format
A For-and-against essay should consist of THREE main parts:
1. Introduction
2. Body
3. Conclusion
 However, a For-and-against Essay always consists of FOUR paragraphs
4. Introduction
5. Arguments for
6. Arguments against
7. Conclusion
Together, these two paragraphs
make up the BODY of the essay.
Each individual part of a for-and-against essay also has
to consist of several smaller parts.
 The Introduction consists of a Introductory statement and a Thesis
statement.
 Both the arguments for and arguments against consist of AT
LEAST TWO individual arguments for or against, respectively.
 The Conclusion consists of a Concluding statement and a
Personal opinion
Essay Introduction 
This part of the essay has 2 main functions:
8. Introduce the reader to the topic in question (Introductory statement)
9. Show the reader that there are two sides to the topic (Thesis statement)
 Let’s look at an example of this with the following essay topic:
Some people say school uniforms should be mandatory. Others disagree.
The title/topic of our essay is the following: Some people say school uniforms should be
mandatory. Others disagree.
An introductory paragraph may look something like this:
School uniforms have been present in schools for several decades now. Their
popularity has changed over time and in recent year the debate has resurfaced
again. While some say that school should pass regulations making school uniforms
compulsory for all students, some say that this wouldn’t be the right course for the
future.
 In this case, the two sentences written in blue are our introductory statement (as you can
see, it doesn’t have to be one single sentence). They tell the reader that the general topic
of our essay is school uniforms. The sentence written in red is our thesis statement. Its job is
to tell the reader that, when it comes to the topic at hand (school uniforms), there are two
views – some are for them, some are against them.
 Quick tip: If you ever get stuck when trying to think of a good thesis statement, just
paraphrase the title of the essay (have it look different, but still saying the same thing).
Essay Conclusion
 Don’t worry! We’ll get to the arguments for and arguments against. Relax!
Why aren’t you relaxing?
 A conclusion of a for-and-against essay consists of two parts:
10. Concluding statement – in which we summarise the main talking points from the
body of the essay (and basically admit that there really are two sides to the
topic in question)
11. Personal opinion – in which we state our opinion on the topic in question. NOTE:
this is the ONLY part of the entire essay in which you are allowed to be
subjective. Here you can say whether you are „for” the topic, „against” it,
maybe somewhere in the middle, or maybe you don’t even care (which is also
a valid opinion to have.
 Let’s look at an example for the same essay topic as before:
In conlcusion, it is clear that there are both positive sides to school uniforms – such as developing a sense of community promoting equality – as well as
negative sides – such as the stiffling of student individualism and their
questionable effectiveness in battling bullying. Personally, I think that school
uniforms could potentially be a good idea, but that there should be room for
students to somehow express themselves through their clothes.
 Here we can easily see where the concluding statement begins, as well as
where the personal opinion begins. We use the appropriate linking words or
linking phrases (popularly known as linkers) to „warn” the reader what will
immediatelly follow those linkers. (We’ll cover linkers in more detail next week, but you can easily think of some alternatives for these two linkers which are used
in the example).
 One other important thing to note is that the arguments you may mention in the
conclusion have to be arguments that you’ve mentioned earlier in the body of
the essay, not completely new arguments. Although, it is not absolutely
necessary to mention those arguments again. The part of the concluding
statemnt written in italics can be left out and it would still be a good concluding
statement.
The Essay Body (Arguments for/against)Today we’ll only cover the basics of writing the body of an essay, and go into more detail
on how to choose, write and refine your arguments next week.
 The main rules/guidelines for writing the body of the essay (or the main TWO paragraphs
are):
12. You need to have the same number of arguments for and arguments against (2 of each, 3
of each…)
13. You need at least 2 of each arguments (for a minimum total of 4 arguments)
14. Each argument has to be supported and explained, and/or given an example which shows
why it’s important
15. Each individual argument has to be introduced by an appropriate linking word/phrase.
16. All arguments have to be presented and explained in an objective and neutral way.
 Again, we’ll get into more detail for this part next week. But these are 5 core rules that you
should always remember when writing the body of a for-and-against essay.
Other General Rules
 LENGTH - To quote an old saying: „An essay should be like a skirt – long
enough to cover the topic, but short enough to keep it interesting.” No-one
is expecting you to write a doctoral disertation here, just to show that you
can explain an argument. Typically high-school argumentative essays are
supposed to be 200-250 words.
 STYLE – be on the N.O.S.E (Neutral, Objective, Succinct (or Simple),
Effective).
The N.O.S.E
 Neutral – Use a neutral writing style. Avoid using informal language (for example, don’t say „kids”,
say „children”) and grammatical structures. Contractions are not strictly forbidden, but some
should be avoided if at all possible (for example „hadn’t”, „must’ve”, „You’ll”, etc.) It’s not a big
mistake if you DO use them, but with time try to phase them out.
 Objective – You can’t show that you favour one side of the argument over the other in any way. This is something that students often have problems with, especially if the topic in question is
something they feel very strongly about. There’s nothing else to say here but: suck it up. Remember, just because you’re writing about something you disagree with doesn’t
automatically mean that you agree with it.
 Succinct/Simple – Avoid wordiness (or tautology). It’s typically used by students who fear that
they will appear as less intelligent if they use everyday words, but then overcompensate and use
a totally unnecessarily complex (or superfluous) word. You can use them sparingly, but avoid
overloading your essay with them. A good rule of thumb is to not use two words when one word
will do.
 Effective – Some students feel that if they overload their essays with 5 arguments for each side
that then the reader will think they’re really really smart. We won’t. The only thing which will
probably happen is that you’ll run out of good arguments and use poor arguments which aren’t
really well explained in your essay. It’s better to have fever arguments which are well explained
and supported than a bunch of them which you won’t cover well enough.
Steps in writing arguments
17. Think about the topic at hand and make a list of possible arguments
18. Analyse your list and choose at least 2 arguments for and 2 arguments against.
19. Write down a first draft of an argument.
20. Refine your argument into its finished form.Step 1 – Making a list
■ When beginning to list the arguments, think about the topic at hand from different
viewpoints and perspectives.
■ If you get stuck, you can always try to use S.P.E.R.M. and go through each
perspective, writing down new potential arguments as you think of them.
■ Remember, you don’t have to use each argument you write down, so don’t worry
whether a potential argument is good or bad or whether you can explain it. You’ll
worry about that later. For now, just write them down.S.P.E.R.M.
■ Social – does the issue raised by the topic affect people from different social classes or
background differently (rich people vs. Poor people, young people vs. Old people,…)
■ Political – is there a political issue related to the topic at hand?
■ Economical – does the issue raised by the topic have a significant economic impact on
people or society?
■ Research – how does the topic at hand affect technology, or is it possibly somehow
affected by technology?
■ Moral – are there any moral/ethical questions which are raised by the topic?
■ REMEMBER: Don’t spend too much time on writing down a list of arguments. This should
take you some 5-8 minutes to do.Step 2 – checking the list (and checking
it twice)
■ Once you have list of potential arguments, you now need to choose at least 2 arguments
from each side (for AND against) which you will present in your essay.
■ There really is no science to this. But there are several guidelines which you should
follow for this step:
• Remember that it’s better to have fewer well-explained arguments than a whole bunch
of poorly reasoned arguments.
• A lot of perspectives on a topic have both a positive and a negative side to them. Try to
find those perspective because they can give you 2 arguments for „the price” of one.
• Only choose those arguments that you can explain why they’re important.
 REMEMBER: Like with thinking of possible arguments, don’t spend too much time here.
Once you have 2 arguments for each side, you should be ready to give up choosing more
if you get stuck. You still have the hardest steps to do, so mind the time.
Step 3 – First draft
■ Each argument consists of two parts:
■ Stating the argument; Explaining the argument
■ When writing an argument down, write something down – no matter how simple a
sentence. Write something down. Remember, it’s only a first draft. You’ll fix it later.
■ Let’s take a quick example argument for school uniforms:
School uniforms promote euqality (this is us stating the argument). They make students look
equal (this is us explaining why the argument is relevant).
■ Let’s be clear. This argument is horribly written. But right this moment it isn’t that
important. What’s important now is that we have AN argument written down. We will fix it
in the next step.
Step 4 – Refining and revision
■ In this (most important step in writing an argument) we actually refine our argument
into something we can put in our final version of our essay. We do this by going back
to our first draft and choosing the correct vocabulary, phrases, and sentence
structures, putting in the correct linkers.
■ In general, this where we’re making our argument presentable.
■ Let’s look back at our first draft
Refining and revision - example

First of all, it is claimed that school uniforms promote equality among students because the uniforms’
identical appearance makes students look equal by hiding their own clothes, which may reflect the socio- economic status.

- The impersonal report structure makes the sentence more objective (since it’s not us saying this, but

- These comments are added for clarity. The first one to show that the equality applies only to students

Ending revision
■ Once you have done this process for all of your arguments, you have successfully
written the body of your essay.
■ Again, whether you’ll write down a first draft for all your arguments first, or you’ll
work and work on one argument before moving on to the next one is up to you. You
need to choose how you proceed here. Each person is different in this regard.
